Travel insurance is one product made of several building blocks. Two policies with the same name can offer very different protection, so it pays to know what each block does and which ones matter for your trip.

## Medical expenses abroad

This is the heart of the policy. It pays for treatment you need because of an illness or accident during the trip: doctors, hospital stays, surgery, medicines and sometimes emergency dental care.

What to check:

- **The limit.** Inside Europe a lower limit can be enough; for the United States, Canada, Japan or long trips, look for very high limits or cover at actual cost.
- **Direct billing.** The best policies pay the hospital directly, so you do not have to advance large sums.
- **The excess.** Some policies leave a fixed amount per claim at your expense.

> The cheapest policy is worth nothing if its medical limit runs out in the first week of a hospital stay.

## Repatriation and medical evacuation

If you are seriously ill or injured, the assistance company organises and pays for your transport home or to the nearest suitable hospital, sometimes by air ambulance. It is often the single most expensive item, which is why the best policies cover it **at actual cost** rather than up to a fixed amount. Most also cover the return of a travelling companion and, in the worst case, the repatriation of the body.

## Trip cancellation

Cancellation cover refunds the non-refundable costs of your trip — flights, accommodation, tours — if you have to cancel before leaving for a covered reason: illness, accident, death of a close relative, serious damage to your home, redundancy and more, depending on the policy.

Two styles exist:

- **Listed reasons**: only the reasons named in the conditions are covered.
- **Any justified reason**: broader cover for any unforeseeable event you can prove.

## Early return and trip interruption

If you must cut your trip short — a relative is hospitalised at home, for example — this cover pays your return ticket and sometimes the unused part of the trip.

## Baggage and delays

Covers lost, stolen or damaged luggage up to a limit, usually with sub-limits for valuables and electronics, plus essential purchases if your bag is delayed. It is rarely generous: if you travel with expensive equipment, check the per-item limits.

## Personal liability

If you accidentally injure someone or damage their property — a skiing collision, a broken item in a rented flat — this cover pays the compensation you owe. Limits are usually high, and it is easy to overlook until you need it.

## 24/7 assistance

The assistance line is what turns a policy into real help: it finds a hospital, guarantees payment, speaks to doctors and organises transport. A good assistance service in your language is worth more than many minor extras.

## Common exclusions

Every policy has exclusions. The most frequent:

- **Pre-existing medical conditions** that are not declared or not accepted.
- **Adventure or extreme sports** without the matching option (skiing off-piste, diving beyond a certain depth, high-altitude trekking).
- Accidents under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
- Travel to countries your government advises against.
- Routine or planned treatment abroad.

That is why every offer on Voyelis links to its **IPID** (the standard product information document) and its general conditions — read them before you buy.

## How to choose

1. Start with **medical cover and repatriation**, sized for your destination.
2. Add **cancellation** if you have paid a lot in advance.
3. Declare the **activities** you will do and the **ages** of all travellers.
4. Only then compare prices.
