When you compare travel insurance, two policies can both say "cancellation included" and still behave very differently on the day you need them. The difference is **which reasons** trigger a refund.

## 1. Named reasons

The policy lists the covered events one by one. Typical examples:

- serious illness, accident or death of the insured person, a travel companion or a close relative;
- complications of pregnancy;
- serious damage to your home or business;
- redundancy, or a summons to court.

**Strengths:** lower premium, low or no excess for medical reasons.
**Weakness:** if your reason is not on the list — a work emergency, a missed connection to the airport, a minor illness that still stops you travelling — the claim is refused. Lists vary a lot: some cards cover only a handful of reasons, some insurers several dozen.

## 2. Any justified reason

Instead of a closed list, the policy accepts **any event** that is:

- **external** and **independent of your will**,
- **sudden** and **unforeseeable** when you bought the policy,
- **provable** with documents,
- and the direct cause of the cancellation.

That covers many situations a list misses. But the key word is **justified**: you still need a real event and evidence. A change of mind is not covered. This option usually comes with a **percentage excess** on the refund.

## 3. Cancel for any reason (CFAR)

No reason and no proof are required, but the refund is **partial** — usually 50% to 75% — and the conditions on timing are strict. See our [CFAR guide](/guides/cancel-for-any-reason-travel-insurance).

## Side by side

| | Named reasons | Any justified reason | Cancel for any reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reason needed | Yes, from the list | Yes, any unforeseeable event | No |
| Proof needed | Yes | Yes | No |
| Typical refund | Up to 100% minus excess | Up to 100% minus excess | 50%–75% |
| Excess | Low | Often a percentage | Built into the partial refund |
| Price | Lowest | Higher | Highest |

## How to choose

1. **What worries you most?** If it is illness or a family emergency, named reasons usually cover it well and cost less.
2. **How complex is your life?** Self-employed, frequent work changes, children, elderly parents: "any justified reason" gives room for the unexpected.
3. **How much is non-refundable?** The more you have paid in advance, the more a broader cover is worth.
4. **Read the excess.** Two "any justified reason" policies can have very different excesses and limits.
