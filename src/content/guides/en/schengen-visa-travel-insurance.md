If you need a short-stay visa to visit Europe, the consulate will ask for proof of travel medical insurance. The rules are the same for all Schengen countries, and an application with the wrong policy is a common reason for delays or refusals.

## Who needs it

Citizens of countries that need a **Schengen short-stay visa** (type C) must provide travel medical insurance with their application. Visa-exempt travellers — from the United Kingdom, the United States or Canada, for example — are not required to show it, but should still be insured.

The Schengen Area currently has 29 countries: most EU members plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Ireland and Cyprus are in the EU but not in Schengen.

## The minimum requirements

The common EU visa rules require a policy that:

- **covers at least €30,000** for medical expenses;
- is valid in **all Schengen countries**, not only the one you visit;
- covers the **whole stay**, from entry to exit;
- includes **emergency medical treatment, hospital care and repatriation** for medical reasons, including in case of death.

Consulates also check that the certificate names every traveller and shows the policy number and dates. A policy with higher limits is always accepted; one with lower limits or a gap in dates is not.

## Common mistakes

- Insuring only the country of the consulate instead of the whole Schengen Area.
- Dates shorter than the planned stay (or not matching the flight reservation).
- Using a credit card's travel cover: it rarely produces a named certificate with the required limits.
- Forgetting a family member on the certificate.

## Beyond the visa

The €30,000 minimum is a legal floor, not a recommendation. A long hospital stay in Western Europe can exceed it. If the price difference is small, prefer higher medical limits and repatriation at actual cost.
