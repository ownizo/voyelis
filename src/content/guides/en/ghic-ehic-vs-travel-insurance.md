Many European travellers believe their health card is enough for a trip in Europe. It is a useful safety net — but it was never designed to replace travel insurance.

## What the cards are

- **EHIC (European Health Insurance Card):** issued free of charge by the national health system of EU countries (and Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland). It gives access to medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay in another of these countries.
- **GHIC (Global Health Insurance Card):** the UK card that replaced the EHIC for most UK residents. It gives access to medically necessary state healthcare in EU countries. Some UK residents can still use a UK EHIC.

With either card you are treated **on the same terms as locals**: if residents pay part of the cost, so do you.

## What they do not cover

- **Private hospitals and clinics** — common in tourist areas, and often where you are taken first.
- **Co-payments** that residents pay themselves.
- **Medical repatriation** to your home country.
- **Mountain rescue** and helicopter evacuation in many countries.
- **Trip cancellation**, early return, baggage and personal liability.
- **Any country outside the scheme** — the USA, Turkey, Morocco, Asia…

## Why use both

The best approach is to carry **your card and a travel insurance policy**. The card can reduce the cost of public treatment; the insurance covers the rest and, above all, organises and pays for repatriation if needed. Some insurers even waive the excess if you used your card for public treatment.

## Practical tips

1. Apply for your card only through the **official website** of your health service — it is free.
2. Check its **expiry date** before you travel.
3. Save your insurer's **assistance number** in your phone.
4. In an emergency, ask the assistance company where to go: a public hospital (where the card is valid) or a private one (where only insurance helps).
