There is no single price for travel insurance: the same week in Spain or in California can cost very different amounts. Here are the factors that move the price — and how to keep it under control without weakening your protection.

## The main price factors

### Destination

Insurers group destinations into zones. Europe is usually the cheapest; worldwide cover costs more, and **the USA and Canada** are often priced separately because of medical costs.

### Trip length

The longer you are away, the higher the premium. For frequent travellers, an [annual multi-trip policy](/guides/annual-multi-trip-travel-insurance) can cost less than several single-trip policies.

### Age of the travellers

Prices increase with age, sometimes sharply above a certain threshold. Children are often cheaper and sometimes covered for free with their parents.

### Cancellation amount

Cancellation cover is priced according to the **trip cost you insure**. It is often the largest part of the premium for an expensive trip.

### Cover level and excess

Higher medical limits, repatriation at actual cost, no excess or options (adventure sports, valuables) increase the price — and the protection.

## How to pay less without losing protection

1. **Compare** several insurers for the same trip: prices for similar cover can differ a lot.
2. **Do not double-insure:** if your card already provides solid medical cover, consider cancellation only.
3. **Insure the real trip cost** for cancellation, no more.
4. **Choose an annual policy** if you travel several times a year.
5. **Accept a reasonable excess** on baggage rather than on medical cover.

## What not to save on

- **Medical limits** for long-haul destinations.
- **Repatriation** at actual cost.
- **Declaring activities and conditions** — an undeclared risk can cost you the whole claim.
