"I just don't feel like going anymore." No standard cancellation policy will pay for that. **Cancel for any reason** (CFAR) is the one type of cover designed for exactly that situation — with conditions you need to know before you rely on it.

## What CFAR is

CFAR is an **optional upgrade** to a full travel insurance policy. It lets you cancel your trip for a reason that standard cancellation cover would not accept — a change of plans, a work commitment, worry about the destination — and still get **part of your money back**.

It exists alongside two other kinds of cancellation cover:

| Type | What triggers a refund | Typical refund |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Named reasons** | Only the events listed in the policy (illness, accident, death of a relative…) | Up to 100% of the insured cost, minus any excess |
| **Any justified reason** | Any unforeseeable event outside your control that you can prove | Up to 100%, often with a higher excess |
| **Cancel for any reason** | No reason needed | Usually **50% to 75%** of the insured cost |

Our guide on [named reasons vs any justified reason](/guides/named-reasons-vs-any-justified-reason-cancellation) explains the first two in detail.

## The rules that apply almost everywhere

CFAR is generous on reasons but strict on timing. The conditions vary by insurer, but they usually look like this:

1. **Buy it early.** CFAR must be bought within a short window after your **first trip payment** — commonly 10 to 21 days. Miss the window and it is gone for that trip.
2. **Insure the full trip cost.** You normally have to insure **100% of your prepaid, non-refundable costs**, not just part of them.
3. **Cancel in time.** Most policies require you to cancel **at least 48 to 72 hours before departure**.
4. **Only the non-refundable part.** Any refund, voucher or credit you receive from the airline or hotel is deducted from the claim.

## How much does it cost?

CFAR is an add-on, so it **raises the premium** of the base policy. Whether it pays off depends on how likely you are to change your mind and how much of the trip is non-refundable.

A quick test: if you cancel, CFAR typically returns 50% to 75%. Compare that refund with the extra premium and with the refund rules of your bookings — a flexible hotel rate or a refundable fare can be a cheaper way to keep your options open.

## When CFAR makes sense

- **Expensive, non-refundable trips** booked far in advance: safaris, cruises, honeymoons, group trips.
- **Uncertain plans**: a job change, a family situation, a destination where the political or health situation might change.
- **Trips where a "named reason" is unlikely to apply** but you still want an exit.

## When it does not

- Cheap or largely refundable trips.
- When your main fear is illness or accident — **standard cancellation cover already refunds up to 100%** for those reasons.
- Last-minute bookings: the purchase window has often already closed.

## Where to find it

CFAR is widespread in the North American market and rarer in Europe, where insurers more often offer **"any justified reason"** cover. Some European insurers have started to offer cancellation **without proof**, usually with a significantly higher excess.
